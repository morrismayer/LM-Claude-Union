## IG Carousel 1 — Pattern repeat: the check nobody does

**Slide 1:** The spec sheet looks right. The swatch looks right. Then the
panels go up and the pattern is off by three inches.

**Slide 2:** Pattern repeat is the yardage variable that gets skipped most
often. A large repeat — big floral, wide stripe — needs extra fabric so the
motif lines up panel to panel.

**Slide 3:** Miss it on the bolt and you can't fix it at install. The
mismatch is visible at eye level from across the room.

**Slide 4:** The fix is simple: calculate for repeat before the fabric
order, not after. Five minutes of math versus a remake.

**Slide 5:** Same rule applies to COM. If you're sourcing your own material,
get the yardage confirmed with your workroom before the bolt ships — not
after it arrives.

## IG Carousel 2 — Trim is three decisions, not one

**Slide 1:** Trim is the detail that makes a piece. It's also the decision
that gets made too late, or not at all.

**Slide 2:** Decision 1: The trim itself. Tape, cord, tassel fringe, banding
— each one reads differently and fabricates differently.

**Slide 3:** Decision 2: How it's applied. Glued is faster. Sewn into the
seam moves with the fabric and holds up over time, especially on pieces that
get real use.

**Slide 4:** Decision 3: Where it sits. A banded inset reads completely
differently than trim right at the leading edge. Both are right for different
rooms, different clients.

**Slide 5:** Make all three decisions before fabrication starts. Trim decided
after the fact usually means a substitution you didn't choose.

## IG Carousel 3 — 5 things a good workroom catches before install day

**Slide 1:** Most window treatment and upholstery problems aren't workroom
problems. They're problems that a better process would have caught earlier.

**Slide 2:** 1. The pattern repeat wasn't checked against the yardage — so
the panels don't match at eye level.

**Slide 3:** 2. The trim application method wasn't decided — so fabrication
used a substitution instead.

**Slide 4:** 3. The fullness ratio was eyeballed, not calculated — so the
fabric looks flat even though it's beautiful.

**Slide 5:** 4. Nobody confirmed whether the hardware was rated for the panel
weight — so install day turns into a callback.
