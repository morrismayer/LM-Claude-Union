Hi {{Contact.FirstName}},

A designer hands me a spec sheet and I can usually tell how the job's going
to go before we cut anything — not from the fabric choice, but from what's
missing around it. Pattern repeat that hasn't been checked against the
yardage. Trim specified without anyone confirming how it'll actually be
applied. Hardware picked for how it looks in a rendering, not how it'll
perform after a few thousand open-close cycles.

None of that is a knock on design training — it just isn't workroom
training. So here's a quick rundown of what we check on every job that comes
through our Mamaroneck workroom:

**Fabric.** Railroaded goods can mean fewer seams on wide panels — but only
some fabrics should be used that way. Large pattern repeats need extra
yardage so motifs line up panel to panel. If you're working with COM
(customer's own material), loop us in before the fabric ships, not after.

**Trim.** It's three decisions, not one: the trim itself, the application
(glued vs. sewn into the seam), and the placement. Make all three early and
trim elevates a piece instead of getting quietly substituted at fabrication.

**Hardware.** Ripplefold track or traditional rod-and-ring with pinch
pleats — both can be right. What's never optional is fullness ratio (how
much fabric width relative to the finished length) and hardware built to
survive daily use, not just look good in a photo.

**Upholstery.** 8-way hand-tied construction ties every spring individually
in eight directions, so a piece performs the same in year five as day one.
We calculate cushion yardage methodically — area to square yards, rounded up,
adjusted for pattern repeat and seam allowance — so a quote doesn't turn into
a change order mid-fabrication.

We do drapery and upholstery under one roof — same workroom, same standards,
same point of contact — with field measuring and installation kept in-house.
One less thing to coordinate on a project that's already asking a lot of your
time.

If you're working on something that could use a workroom partner who checks
these details before they become problems, I'd love to talk it through.

If you have questions or need clarity, I'm here to help—just reach out.
