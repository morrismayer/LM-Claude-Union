## Core voice spec (from BrandScribe)

- **Tone:** Calm, confident, expert.
- **Focus:** Educational, solution-driven, practical — every piece should
  leave the reader knowing something useful, not just impressed.
- **Audience:** Interior designers, hospitality clients, industry peers — the
  trade, first and always, even on consumer-facing channels.
- **Required sign-off line**, used to close long-form pieces (blog, email):
  > "If you have questions or need clarity, I'm here to help—just reach out."

## Patterns confirmed from real published samples

- **First person, owner-operator.** Morris speaking, not a brand committee —
  decades of hands-on trade experience earn the directness.
- **Structure:** a short scene-setting intro, then several H2 sections each
  tackling one concrete, practical sub-topic (e.g. material costs/tariffs,
  a market shift like the hospitality pivot, a named industry trend, the
  actual measure→fabricate→install workflow) — not one undifferentiated wall
  of text.
- **Trade-insider, not tourist.** Real terms used in passing, not dumbed down:
  railroaded goods, COM, 8-way hand-tied, fullness ratio, ripplefold track,
  pinch pleat — plus real vendor names where accurate (Samuel and Sons, Romo,
  Calico).
- **Plainspoken over flowery.** Short, confident sentences. Cuts marketing
  fluff ("elevate your space," "timeless elegance") in favor of specifics —
  lead times, seam allowances, what actually fails first on cheap hardware.
- **Specific over generic.** Reaches for the real detail (6,000 sq ft
  Mamaroneck workroom, the Metro-North pickup, decades of measuring rooms)
  instead of generic adjectives.
- **Warm but efficient.** Friendly to designers as peers/partners, not
  customers to be upsold. No hard-sell language — the sign-off line carries
  the entire CTA weight; the body stays educational.

## Brand voice blend (from the official Plaza-park-dossier)

- 40% high-end and polished
- 40% relationship-driven and trust-building
- 20% direct and no-nonsense

Sounds like: a seasoned project partner. Calm under pressure. Capable,
detail-aware, and reassuring. Experienced enough to solve problems without
drama.

Does NOT sound like: hype-heavy sales copy, generic luxury marketing language,
cheap transactional vendor language, overpromising or vague reassurance.

## Phrases to use (from the dossier — reach for these in sales/messaging)

- "We'll make it work."
- "We'll take care of it."
- "Let's walk through the details."
- "We've handled more complex installs than this."
- "Meeting the deadline won't be an issue, as long as there aren't delays
  outside of our control."
- (BrandScribe required sign-off for long-form) "If you have questions or
  need clarity, I'm here to help—just reach out."
- (Video/social outro) "I'm Morris Mayer. 38 years in custom window treatments
  and upholstery at Plaza Park Interiors in Mamaroneck. [specific CTA]."

## Phrases to AVOID (dossier explicit avoid list)

- "Affordable"
- "Competitive pricing"
- "Industry-leading"
- "We'll try"
- "Premium solutions"
- "White glove" (unless the audience member used that phrase first)
- Generic luxury adjectives with no specifics attached ("luxurious," "elevated,"
  "timeless elegance," "world-class")

## Facebook/social post format from real published output

FB posts in Morris's own system are **conversation-starters**, not
educational broadcasts. They pose a question to the audience ("How far in
advance are you currently getting window treatment work into your schedule?"),
share what Morris is seeing from his side, and invite discussion — soft CTAs
only. Do NOT write FB posts as one-way announcements; write them as designer-
to-designer peer conversations.

IG posts are multi-slide **carousels** (5–6 slides per post), not single
captions. Each slide gets its own short-form copy. The final slide carries the
CTA/contact info.

## Target personas (from the dossier)

**Dana (Deadline-Driven Designer):** Managing multiple projects, tired of
missed deadlines and sloppy follow-up. Cares about reliable execution, clear
communication, a partner who reduces risk and protects her reputation.

**Daniel (Detail-Obsessed Designer):** High-end designer with a strong design
POV who needs a workroom that can execute difficult custom concepts without
watering them down. Cares about technical capability and precision execution.

**Olivia (Hotel Operations Manager):** Hospitality operator needing cleaning,
upkeep, and execution support without disrupting property operations. Cares
about minimal disruption, organized coordination, longevity and maintenance
planning.

## Guardrails

- Audience is **the design trade** first (interior designers, decorators,
  hospitality purchasers) — content should read designer-to-designer, even
  when it's posted somewhere homeowners will also see it.
- Don't claim certifications, specific pricing, or capacity that haven't been
  confirmed (see "Still open" list in `plaza-park-interiors.md`). The pricing
  anchors in that file are internal positioning only — not for quoting.
- Reference PPI Clean when maintenance, longevity, hospitality, or lifecycle
  support is relevant — it's part of the Plaza Park full-lifecycle value prop,
  not a brand to avoid mentioning.
- Keep Plaza Park Decor (retail storefront) out of trade-workroom content
  unless explicitly cross-brand.
- Never surface real client names, project addresses, or financial figures
  found in internal docs (invoices, estimates, triage reports) in public
  content — those are operational records, not marketing material.
- Close long-form pieces (blog post, email) with the BrandScribe sign-off
  line verbatim. Close video scripts with the Morris intro/outro format.
