## Core voice spec (from BrandScribe)

- **Tone:** Calm, confident, expert.
- **Focus:** Educational, solution-driven, practical — every piece should
  leave the reader knowing something useful, not just impressed.
- **Audience:** Interior designers, hospitality clients, industry peers — the
  trade, first and always, even on consumer-facing channels.
- **Required sign-off line**, used to close long-form pieces (blog, email):
  > "If you have questions or need clarity, I'm here to help—just reach out."

## Patterns confirmed from real published samples

- **First person, owner-operator.** Morris speaking, not a brand committee —
  decades of hands-on trade experience earn the directness.
- **Structure:** a short scene-setting intro, then several H2 sections each
  tackling one concrete, practical sub-topic (e.g. material costs/tariffs,
  a market shift like the hospitality pivot, a named industry trend, the
  actual measure→fabricate→install workflow) — not one undifferentiated wall
  of text.
- **Trade-insider, not tourist.** Real terms used in passing, not dumbed down:
  railroaded goods, COM, 8-way hand-tied, fullness ratio, ripplefold track,
  pinch pleat — plus real vendor names where accurate (Samuel and Sons, Romo,
  Calico).
- **Plainspoken over flowery.** Short, confident sentences. Cuts marketing
  fluff ("elevate your space," "timeless elegance") in favor of specifics —
  lead times, seam allowances, what actually fails first on cheap hardware.
- **Specific over generic.** Reaches for the real detail (6,000 sq ft
  Mamaroneck workroom, the Metro-North pickup, decades of measuring rooms)
  instead of generic adjectives.
- **Warm but efficient.** Friendly to designers as peers/partners, not
  customers to be upsold. No hard-sell language — the sign-off line carries
  the entire CTA weight; the body stays educational.

## Guardrails

- Audience is **the design trade** first (interior designers, decorators,
  hospitality purchasers) — content should read designer-to-designer, even
  when it's posted somewhere homeowners will also see it.
- Don't claim certifications, pricing, or capacity that haven't been
  confirmed (see "Still open" list in `plaza-park-interiors.md`).
- Keep Plaza Park Decor (retail arm) and Plaza Park Cleaning Services (separate
  legal entity) out of trade-workroom content unless a piece is explicitly
  cross-brand.
- Never surface real client names, project addresses, or financial figures
  found in internal docs (invoices, estimates, triage reports) in public
  content — those are operational records, not marketing material.
- Close long-form pieces (blog post, email) with the required sign-off line
  above, verbatim.
